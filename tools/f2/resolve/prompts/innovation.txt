You compare two records of Thai research innovations, taken from public innovation catalogues, and decide whether they describe the SAME innovation (one specific technology, product, formula, process or system) or DIFFERENT innovations.

Decide "same" when both records describe the same specific output: the same distinctive mechanism, formula, design or configuration, even if titles are worded differently, the records come from different catalogues, or they report different roles, readiness levels (TRL), prices, provinces or deployment context. Those are source-context differences, not evidence of a different innovation.

Decide "different" when:
- the stated outputs, subjects, raw materials or products differ; or
- the records describe explicit variants of a related idea: a different version, model, size, capacity, number of parts/compartments, formula or configuration (for example heated vs unheated, V1 vs improved version, 2-compartment vs 3-compartment).
A shared researcher, institution, programme or project does NOT by itself make two records the same innovation; one team often produces several related innovations. A shared reported IP / application number is NOT sufficient by itself either: catalogues sometimes reuse an identifier across unrelated outputs.

Catalogue copy errors: if the two titles name incompatible outputs (for example a curry-paste grinder vs a plastic shredder, or a biomass stove vs a solar dryer), decide "different" even when the descriptions, figures or IP numbers match. A description that contradicts its own record's title was copied from another innovation by mistake; it is not evidence that the two records are the same innovation.

Granularity: one innovation is one specific output at one design, size, version and formulation.

Not reasons to decide "different":
- Differences in role, TRL, price, deployment site, project, province or target users, or minor operating figures reported for the same design in different catalogues (e.g. drying at 50-55 C vs 60-80 C).
- The material or crop a machine is applied to: a grinder or press is one innovation whatever it processes.

Decide "different" for:
- Size, capacity or configuration stated as part of the identity (small vs large, 2 vs 3 compartments, 1 vs 2 doors), or two listings whose key specifications differ materially (motor power, throughput, capacity). This applies only when BOTH records state the size, configuration or specification and they conflict; if only one record mentions it, that is not a reason to decide "different".
- Different versions (original vs improved, V1 vs V2, manual vs automated).
- A mould, kit or tool for making X versus X itself. In Thai, "แบบหล่อเตา..." is a mould and "เตา...แบบหล่อ" is a cast stove.
- For processes, formulas and products: a different crop, raw material, key ingredient or target animal.
- Formulas: the number does not matter, the ingredients do. "สูตร2 (ต้นกล้วยสับ)" is the same as "...ด้วยต้นกล้วยสับ" and different from "...ด้วยมูลวัว".
- A part versus the whole, when the part is listed as its own output (artificial comb vs bee house).
- A process versus equipment or material used in it (insemination technique vs semen diluent).
- A programme bundle versus one technology inside it.

Decide "unsure" when one record is too generic or title-only to apply these rules, including a generic record versus a specific variant (generic pra-seed milk vs black-sesame pra-seed milk). More generally, decide "unsure" when the evidence cannot settle it, typically because one record is title-only or generic and lacks the details needed to confirm or rule out a match, or the shared text is generic programme wording used across many technologies.

Confidence: "high" only if the records leave no reasonable doubt; otherwise "medium" or "low".
Give evidence as one or two short English sentences naming the decisive detail.
